How to open iPhone HEIC photos on Windows

You copied your photos from the iPhone to your PC and Windows won't open them, or shows them as unknown files with no thumbnail? They are HEIC photos. Here is what they are, what Windows 10 and 11 need to show them, and how to turn them into JPEG without losing date and location.

What HEIC photos are

HEIC is the format the iPhone has used for photos since iOS 11 (2017), with the default “High Efficiency” setting. It is a HEIF image compressed with HEVC (H.265), the same technology used by modern video. Many Android phones, drones and several cameras use it too.

  • It takes about half the space of a JPEG of similar quality, which is why your phone fills up more slowly.
  • It keeps more information than JPEG: 10-bit color, HDR photos, portrait depth.
  • Live Photos are a HEIC photo plus a short .MOV video with the same name.

It is not a worse format: it is just less compatible than JPEG, especially with Windows and older programs.

Why Windows can't open them

Windows reads HEIC only when two Microsoft Store extensions are installed:

  • HEIF Image Extensions: free, it teaches Windows the file format.
  • HEVC Video Extensions: decodes the compression. Many PCs ship with it from the manufacturer; otherwise it may be a small paid item in the Store.

With both installed, HEIC photos open in the Photos app, get thumbnails in File Explorer and work in Paint and in any program that uses the Windows codecs, WFastPic included.

How to check whether you already have them

Open Settings → Apps → Installed apps and search for “HEIF” and “HEVC”. Or open a HEIC photo with the Photos app: if something is missing, Windows tells you and offers to get it.

Install the HEIF and HEVC extensions

  1. Open the Microsoft Store, search for “HEIF Image Extensions” (publisher Microsoft Corporation) and click Get.
  2. Search for “HEVC Video Extensions” (also by Microsoft Corporation) and install it.
  3. Close and reopen File Explorer (or restart the PC) to see the thumbnails.

Beware of sites offering “free HEVC codecs” as .exe or .appx downloads: install the extensions only from the Microsoft Store, published by Microsoft Corporation.

iPhone settings: HEIC or JPEG

If you would rather avoid HEIC altogether, the iPhone has two settings.

Shoot JPEG directly

Settings → Camera → Formats → “Most Compatible”. From then on new photos are JPEG (and videos H.264). Photos you already took stay HEIC. The cost: files about twice as large, and some video modes are only available with “High Efficiency”.

Convert while copying to the PC

Settings → Photos → “Transfer to Mac or PC”: with “Automatic” the iPhone converts photos to JPEG while you copy them over the cable; with “Keep Originals” you get the HEIC files as they are, with full quality and less space. If your PC has the extensions, “Keep Originals” is the better choice.

Copy photos from iPhone to PC

  • With the cable: connect the iPhone, unlock it and tap “Allow”. Then open the Windows Photos app → Import, or in File Explorer open Apple iPhone → Internal Storage → DCIM and copy the folders.
  • With iCloud: the iCloud for Windows app syncs your photo library to a folder on the PC.

Next to every Live Photo you will find a .MOV file with the same name: that is the short video. Delete it and only the photo remains.

Convert HEIC to JPEG

JPEG is what you need to send photos to someone, upload them to a site that doesn't accept HEIC, print them or open them in an old program.

One photo at a time

With the extensions installed, open the photo in Paint → File → Save as → JPEG. Fine for one or two photos, but this usually drops the shooting data (date, location).

Online converters

They are handy, but you upload your photos to someone else's server, often with the GPS location of where you took them. For personal photos a program that works on your PC is better.

Questions

Are HEIC photos lower quality than JPEG?

No, quite the opposite: at the same size they look better, and at the same quality they take about half the space.

Can I delete the HEIC files after converting them to JPEG?

You can, but it is better not to: JPEG loses some quality and the HDR information. The HEIC files are the originals.

Why do the colors of the converted JPEG look duller?

The iPhone shoots in the Display P3 color space, wider than the sRGB used by JPEG. If the converter ignores the color profile, colors get duller. WFastPic converts them to sRGB on export.

What about the iPhone's .MOV videos?

They are often HEVC too: with the HEVC Video Extensions you can watch them in the Windows Media Player app.

Do I need the extensions on Windows 11 too?

Yes, it is the same mechanism. Many new PCs have them preinstalled.
