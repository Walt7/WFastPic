Picasa alternatives in 2026

For many people Picasa was the easiest way to look at, pick and fix their photos. Google shut it down in 2016 and nothing has been quite as straightforward since. Here is what you can use on Windows today, with honest pros and cons — including WFastPic, the program I wrote precisely to get Picasa back.

What happened to Picasa

In February 2016 Google announced the end of Picasa: from March the desktop program was no longer developed, and Picasa Web Albums closed shortly after. Google pointed users to Google Photos, which is an online service, not a program for the folders on your PC.

The last version, Picasa 3.9, can still be found on archive sites and sometimes runs on Windows 10 and 11, but:

  • it has had no updates, not even security fixes, since 2016;
  • it cannot read HEIC photos from iPhones and many of today's phones, nor RAW files from recent cameras;
  • all the online features (web albums, sharing, Google account sign-in) no longer work;
  • downloading it from third-party sites is a risk: there is no official source any more.

What made Picasa special

To know what to look for in an alternative, it helps to remember why people loved Picasa:

  • it worked on your own folders, as they were on disk, without importing anything into a catalog;
  • folders were grouped by year: you found the 2009 vacation in a moment;
  • it was very fast even with thousands of photos;
  • fixes took one click: “I'm Feeling Lucky”, crop, straighten, never damaging the original;
  • it recognized faces and grouped them by person, and put photos on a map;
  • it exported resized photos to send or print;
  • it was free and simple: no course needed to use it.

No program today does all of this in exactly the same way. Each covers a part of it.

The alternatives, one by one

Google Photos

Google's online service · free up to 15 GB (shared with Gmail and Drive), then paid with Google One

  • Pros: photos on every device, search by content (“beach”, “dog”), faces, shared albums, automatic backup from the phone.
  • Cons: photos must live on Google's servers; it does not work on the folders on your PC; with many photos the free space runs out quickly.

Microsoft Photos

Preinstalled in Windows 10 and 11 · free

  • Pros: already there, opens HEIC with the Windows extensions, basic edits, connects to iCloud and OneDrive.
  • Cons: made for viewing, not for quickly picking from hundreds of photos; organization and culling tools are much simpler than Picasa's.

digiKam

Open source · free · Windows, macOS, Linux

  • Pros: the most complete free option: tags, ratings, face recognition, map, advanced search, editing, RAW and HEIC.
  • Cons: many features mean a complex interface and a learning curve; it keeps its own database that must stay in sync.

XnView MP

Free for private use · Windows, macOS, Linux

  • Pros: very fast viewer that opens hundreds of formats, great for batch converting and renaming.
  • Cons: more a viewer and converter than an organizer: no people like in Picasa; an interface packed with options.

FastStone Image Viewer

Free for personal use · Windows only

  • Pros: quick and light, with editing and batch conversion tools; works on your folders.
  • Cons: it works like a file browser for images: no folders by year, people or map; a somewhat dated look.

Adobe Lightroom Classic

Subscription · Windows and macOS

  • Pros: the photographers' standard: professional-grade culling, catalog and RAW development.
  • Cons: a monthly subscription, a learning curve, and photos must be imported into a catalog. Often too much for vacation photos.

WFastPic

Open source (MIT) · free · Windows 10 and 11 · written by the author of this page

  • Pros: built on purpose as a tribute to Picasa: works on your folders, grouped by year; Space to keep a photo, stars, “I'm Feeling Lucky”, crop with suggested crops that never cut faces, straighten; people with names shared across folders, map, JPEG export keeping date and GPS. Made for HEIC photos from iPhones and drones, recognizes bursts and Live Photos. Everything on your PC, no account.
  • Cons: Windows only; a young project (version 1.0, October 2026); no collages, slideshows or web albums, no video playback, no advanced photo editing; HEIC needs the Windows HEIF and HEVC extensions; not yet digitally signed, so Windows asks for confirmation on first start.

Which one to choose

  • You want your photos on phone, PC and tablet without thinking about it → Google Photos.
  • You just look at photos now and then → Microsoft Photos, which you already have.
  • You have a huge archive and want to tag everything carefully → digiKam.
  • You mostly need to open and convert many formats → XnView MP or FastStone.
  • You shoot RAW and want full control → Lightroom Classic.
  • You are back from a trip with hundreds of HEIC photos and want to keep the best, fix them and export them fast, like you did with Picasa → WFastPic.

Moving from Picasa to WFastPic

There is nothing to import: WFastPic opens your folders as they are and groups them by shooting year. Selections, stars and edits are saved in a hidden .wfastpic folder next to the photos, never touching the originals.

The stars, face names and edits you made in Picasa (in the .picasa.ini files) are not imported: all your photos are there, but that information has to be redone.
